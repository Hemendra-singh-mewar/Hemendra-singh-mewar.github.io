RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Materials and measurements
for quieter detectors.

My research connects the properties of monocrystalline silicon with the experimental challenges of operating precision instruments at cryogenic temperatures.

01 / MATERIALS

Silicon characterisation

Investigating the structural, electrical, thermal and optical properties of monocrystalline silicon for cryogenic test masses.

ResistivityCrystal qualityBirefringence
02 / MECHANICS

Mechanical loss

Developing experiments to study mechanical dissipation and quality factors, including electrostatic excitation and ringdown measurements.

Q factorElectrostatic driveThermal noise
03 / OPTICS

Optical thermometry

Exploring how changes in silicon's optical path length can reveal its bulk temperature as it cools.

InterferometryRefractive indexThermal expansion

ABOUT ME

Engineering foundations.
A physicist's curiosity.

I am a PhD researcher at the University of Liège, working in the Precision Mechatronics Laboratory. My focus is on silicon materials and precision measurement for cryogenic gravitational wave detector technology.

My background spans mechanical engineering, physics and astrophysics. I completed an MSc in Astrophysics at Cardiff University before beginning my doctoral research in Liège.

I enjoy connecting physical models with practical experiments: understanding what a measurement tells us, where its limitations lie and how to improve it.
