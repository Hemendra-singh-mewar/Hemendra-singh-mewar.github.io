RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Thermal noise:
from material to detector.

My research brings together silicon characterisation, mechanical loss measurements and optical thermometry to understand the material and temperature dependence of detector thermal noise.

01 / MATERIALS

Silicon characterisation

Characterising crystal quality, electrical properties and optical behaviour to inform studies of absorption, heating and dissipation. These measurements support the material model needed to assess thermal noise.

ResistivityCrystal qualityBirefringence
02 / MECHANICS

Mechanical loss

Developing electrostatic excitation and ringdown experiments to measure mechanical quality factors. Through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, mechanical loss connects directly to Brownian thermal noise; detector predictions also require the relevant geometry and frequency dependence.

Q factorElectrostatic driveThermal noise
03 / OPTICS

Optical thermometry

Using the silicon test mass itself as an optical cavity to explore bulk thermometry. Tracking thermal expansion and refractive index changes helps establish the temperature conditions needed to interpret loss measurements and thermal noise.

InterferometryRefractive indexThermal expansion

WHY THERMAL NOISE MATTERS

Small fluctuations.
A fundamental sensitivity limit.

Thermally driven motion in mirrors and suspensions can mask a gravitational wave signal. Mechanical dissipation sets the associated Brownian noise through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem. Temperature fluctuations can also change dimensions and refractive index. My work within ET CRISTAL studies the material properties and experimental conditions needed to understand these contributions in cryogenic silicon.
