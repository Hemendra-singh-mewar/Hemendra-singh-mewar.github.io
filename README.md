# Hemendra Singh Mewar

**PhD researcher · Precision Mechatronics Laboratory · University of Liège**

[Visit my academic website](https://hemendra-singh-mewar.github.io/)

I work on cryogenic silicon and precision measurement for gravitational wave detectors. My research within ET CRISTAL connects material characterisation, mechanical dissipation and bulk thermometry with the problem of thermal noise.

## Research

- **Mechanical loss and thermal noise:** electrostatic excitation and ringdown experiments to investigate silicon quality factors, with attention to damping introduced by the measurement environment.
- **Cryogenic optical thermometry:** using silicon as an optical cavity to investigate bulk temperature through changes in thermal expansion and refractive index.
- **Silicon characterisation:** studying electrical, optical and structural properties to inform investigations of absorption, heating and dissipation.
- **Suspension materials:** exploring the relationship between strength, heat extraction and mechanical loss in silicon suspension elements.

Thermal noise is the common motivation. Mechanical loss measurements inform Brownian noise models, while material characterisation and thermometry help establish the properties and operating conditions those models require.

## Selected projects

### [Silicon ray optics simulator](https://github.com/Hemendra-singh-mewar/silicon-slab-ray-tracing-simulator)
Interactive ray tracing and reflection geometry for a plane parallel silicon slab, supporting the design of optical measurements.
[Open the simulator](https://silicon-ray-optics.streamlit.app/)

### [Silicon resistivity mapping](https://github.com/Hemendra-singh-mewar/Resistivity_Measurement)
A proposed measurement layout for comparing resistivity across cylindrical silicon samples. Electrical characterisation supports investigations of absorption and heating; resistivity alone is not a measure of thermal noise.

### Silicon modelling with LAMMPS
A proposed atomistic modelling direction to explore elastic response and heat transport in silicon. The aim is to connect microscopic material behaviour with inputs to thermal noise models, with particular attention to the limits of classical simulations at cryogenic temperatures.

### Laser heating and thermal compensation
My earlier MSc work at Cardiff University investigated shorter wavelength laser heating for thermal compensation using COMSOL, linking optical absorption, heat deposition and temperature distributions.
