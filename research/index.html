A CONNECTED RESEARCH PATH

Energy, materials and precision measurement

My background in mechanical and thermal engineering led me into renewable energy, solar thermal systems and photovoltaic technology. Earlier work on silicon solar panels, including transparent and flexible designs, brought material properties and practical energy systems into the same set of questions.

That experience now informs my approach to cryogenic gravitational wave detectors. In ET CRISTAL, I study monocrystalline silicon through mechanical loss, optical properties and bulk thermometry. The work draws on solid state physics, semiconductor physics, materials science and thermal physics to understand how a material behaves in a precision experiment.

My MSc in astrophysics and work with gravitational wave data provide the astronomical context. The research projects below show how these interests developed, from solar energy and planetary science to cryogenics and detector instrumentation.

EINSTEIN TELESCOPE · CURRENT RESEARCH

ET CRISTAL and cryogenic silicon

At the University of Liège’s Precision Mechatronics Laboratory, my work within ET CRISTAL brings together silicon characterisation, mechanical loss measurements and optical bulk thermometry for cryogenic gravitational wave detectors.

These experiments address thermal noise through the properties of the test mass, its suspension and its measurement environment. They connect cryogenics and thermal physics with precision measurement.

OPTICAL MODELLING

Silicon slab ray tracing

An interactive ray tracing tool for separating front and back surface reflections in silicon. It supports the optical geometry of bulk thermometry, which helps establish the operating temperature used in loss and thermal noise studies.

Explore the repository
MATERIAL CHARACTERISATION

Resistivity mapping

A proposed four-point probe measurement plan for comparing electrical uniformity across silicon samples. Resistivity provides context for carrier-related absorption and heating studies; it does not by itself determine mechanical loss or thermal noise.

Explore the repository
ATOMISTIC MODELLING · RESEARCH DIRECTION

Silicon modelling with LAMMPS

A proposed molecular dynamics study of silicon, exploring how atomic structure and temperature affect elastic response and heat transport. The aim is to connect microscopic behaviour with material inputs for thermal noise models.

Classical molecular dynamics requires careful validation at cryogenic temperatures, where quantum effects become important. A thermal conductivity calculation alone is not a prediction of detector Brownian noise.

LAMMPS thermal transport methods
CARDIFF UNIVERSITY · MSc PROJECT

Thermal compensation for gravitational wave detectors

My Cardiff MSc project investigated shorter wavelength laser heating for thermal compensation systems (TCS), using COMSOL to model absorption, heat deposition and temperature distributions in interferometer optics.

The study explored heating in the 2 to 5 µm range and its application to correcting thermal distortion. It provided the thermal modelling background that now informs my work on cryogenic silicon and detector sensitivity.

EXPERIMENT DEVELOPMENT

Electrostatic excitation and ringdown

Developing a non-contact drive for mechanical loss measurements in silicon. Separating intrinsic material loss from suspension, gas and actuator-related damping is essential before using measured quality factors to inform thermal noise models.

CRYOGENIC SUSPENSIONS

Monocrystalline silicon suspension fibres

Research interests in monocrystalline silicon suspension elements bring together strength, surface quality, heat extraction and mechanical dissipation. The challenge is to support and cool a test mass while limiting suspension thermal noise.

ASTROBIOLOGY · PLANETARY SCIENCE

Planetary formation, exocontinents and habitability

During my Cardiff microproject with Dr Jane Greaves, I explored how the composition and thermal evolution of rocky exoplanets could influence continental formation and habitability.

The work brought together stellar elemental abundances, radiogenic heating, mantle convection and thermal strain. It connected planetary interior modelling with the astrobiological question of which environments might support life.

ASTROPHYSICS · DATA ANALYSIS

Gravitational wave signals in detector noise

My gravitational wave data analysis work used open detector data to explore signal structure, noise spectra and comparisons with model waveforms.

Working with Python and PyCBC connected waveform modelling with practical questions about extracting weak signals from noisy measurements. This complements my present focus on the physical sources of detector noise.

SOLAR ENGINEERING · MATERIALS

Towards transparent crystalline solar panels

My earlier solar engineering work explored approaches to making crystalline solar panels transparent, with an interest in integrating energy generation into the built environment.

This forms part of my work on sustainable solar technologies and related patents, connecting material design with the practical demands of green engineering.
