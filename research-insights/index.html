01 / DISSIPATION

Mechanical loss and thermal noise

Developing electrostatic excitation and ringdown experiments to measure mechanical quality factors. Through the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, mechanical loss connects directly to Brownian thermal noise; detector predictions also require the relevant geometry and frequency dependence.

Ringdown measurements track how a vibration decays after excitation is removed. The measured quality factor describes the total damping of that mode. Separating material loss from suspension, gas and actuator contributions is therefore an essential part of interpreting an experiment.

A high quality factor is useful evidence, but a detector noise prediction also needs the material loss at the relevant frequencies and the way the detector senses motion.

Further reading: Saulson, Thermal noise in mechanical experiments

02 / TEMPERATURE

A silicon test mass as a thermometer

Using the silicon test mass itself as an optical cavity to explore bulk thermometry. Tracking thermal expansion and refractive index changes helps establish the temperature conditions needed to interpret loss measurements and thermal noise.

Light reflected from the front and back surfaces carries information about the optical path through the silicon. That path depends on both the physical thickness and the refractive index, so temperature changes affect both quantities.

Converting the optical signal into temperature requires a calibrated model and careful treatment of other influences, including mechanical strain and laser heating. The objective is to understand the temperature of the material whose mechanical loss is being measured.

03 / MATERIALS

What material measurements tell us

Characterising crystal quality, electrical properties and optical behaviour to inform studies of absorption, heating and dissipation. These measurements support the material model needed to assess thermal noise.

Different measurements answer different questions. Resistivity can provide context for carrier-related absorption and heating. Crystal characterisation and birefringence measurements reveal other aspects of the sample. None is a substitute for measuring mechanical dissipation.

The value comes from bringing these observations together to establish a consistent material model, with the limitations of each measurement made explicit.
